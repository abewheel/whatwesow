# Beehive artwork

The permanent wild hive uses `wild-beehive-spring.png`, `wild-beehive-summer.png`, `wild-beehive-fall.png`, and `wild-beehive-winter.png`. These transparent sprites show a compact oak with a prominent natural golden nest hanging below a side branch. They display at 144 × 144 pixels, with the roots 16 pixels below the projected exterior corner. Foreground woodland leaves this tree clear. The four source cells are cropped to visible alpha bounds and reduced for shipping.

Created with the built-in image generation tool using `farm-atlas.png` as a style reference. Prompt: a two-column, two-row atlas repeating the same gnarled small oak, with a large golden-brown ridged natural hive, dark entrance, and tiny bees, all in the reference's detailed painted pixel style and 2:1 isometric camera. Spring has light green leaves, blossoms, and flowers; summer has dense deep green leaves; fall has amber and russet leaves and leaf litter; winter has bare snow-capped branches, snowy roots and hive, and no flying bees. Preserve the camera, tree, nest, root pivot, and scale. Transparent background, generous gutters, no labels, timber hive boxes, roofs, jars, or other man-made objects.

The buildable wooden hive uses `beehive.png` and `beehive-winter.png`. These existing transparent sprites display at 88 × 104 pixels, centered on a 2 × 2 lot, with feet 24 pixels below the lot center. The renderer, alpha hit test, action menu, and depth sorting share these bounds in `src/game/hive-artwork.ts`. Pending construction fades the artwork and adds timber scaffolding.

The wooden sprites were created with the built-in image generation tool. The starter tent was the first style reference. Prompt: one rustic honey-colored timber beehive with a teal pitched roof, short legs, landing board, dark entrance, honey jar, and two tiny bees; warm detailed painted pixel materials, crisp outlines, upper-left lighting, and a fixed 2:1 isometric camera. Single sprite on a transparent background, with no text or scenery. The winter prompt used the finished wooden hive as reference: preserve geometry, camera, colors, jar, and proportions, add snow to the roof, landing board, and feet, and remove flying bees.
